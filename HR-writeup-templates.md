# Write-up templates, grounded in the 2025-2027 handbook

**For review before anything is wired into the hub.** These become legal records, so the
wording is yours to approve, not mine to assume.

Every quote below was checked against `2025-2027 Employee Handbook.pdf` and carries its
section code, so anyone reading a notice can find the policy in seconds.

---

## What changed from the earlier drafts, and why

**Three quoted passages do not exist in the current handbook.** They came from the
2024-2025 manual. Quoting a policy that no longer exists is what unravels a notice if it is
ever challenged:

- "Caregivers are expected to effectively communicate with all 'customers'…"
- "Employees shall not discuss personal issues or religious or political beliefs…"
- "Good communication between you, the office, your client, and other caregivers…"

**The gift language was the opposite of current policy.** The drafts said employees may
never accept gifts. Section **4Q** says small tokens may occasionally be appropriate and
prohibits something narrower. Writing someone up for accepting a small gift while quoting a
blanket ban you do not have is a real risk. Corrected below.

**The call-off rule is real but softer than drafted.** Section 2 says "whenever possible"
and "whenever reasonably possible", not "required". The notices now say what the handbook
says.

**The action levels were wrong.** The handbook ladder (**2V**) is:

> Coaching · Verbal Warning · Written Warning · Final Written Warning · Suspension · Termination

The drafts offered "Probation", which is not in the handbook. The hub currently offers
Verbal, Written, Final Written and Termination Review, so it is **missing Coaching and
Suspension**. Worth adding so the form matches the policy.

The handbook also says: *"The company reserves the right to skip any step depending upon the
seriousness of the policy violation."* Useful line to keep on the form.

## Shared skeleton

Every notice uses the same bones. Only the middle three parts change per template.

1. **Header** — employee, position, date of incident, date of notice, supervisor
2. **Action level** — from the 2V ladder above
3. **Policy violated** ← *varies*, with the handbook quote and section code
4. **What happened** ← *varies*, checkboxes plus free text
5. **Why it matters** ← *varies*
6. **What we expect now** — numbered, specific, immediate
7. **Employee comments** — left blank, filled by the employee
8. **Acknowledgment** — "My signature does not necessarily indicate agreement with the
   contents of this notice." Employee, supervisor, optional witness.

## 1. Short-notice call-off

**Policy (handbook §2U, Attendance & Dependability):**
> Employees are expected to: Report to work as scheduled. Arrive on time. Complete assigned
> shifts. Notify the office immediately if attendance issues arise. Maintain reliable attendance.

**Notice requirement (handbook, Section 2):**
> Whenever possible, notify the office at least four (4) hours before the beginning of your
> shift. For shifts beginning between 7:00 AM and 9:00 AM, notify the office no later than
> 5:00 PM the previous day, whenever reasonably possible.

**Checkboxes:** ☐ Less than 4 hours' notice ☐ 7-9 AM shift, not notified by 5 PM prior
☐ Repeated short-notice call-offs ☐ Other

**Why it matters:** clients depend on scheduled care for safety and daily living. Short
notice makes replacement coverage difficult and can leave a vulnerable client without the
support they were promised.

**Expected now:** report to assigned shifts; give as much notice as circumstances allow;
call the office directly rather than texting a coworker; keep transportation arrangements
reliable; consider availability carefully before accepting assignments.

## 2. No Call / No Show  ⚠ serious

**Policy (handbook §2U):**
> A No Call / No Show occurs when an employee fails to report to work and fails to notify the
> company. Because this places vulnerable clients at significant risk, No Call / No Show
> incidents are considered serious misconduct and may result in immediate termination.

⚠ **Handle differently.** 2V lists No Call / No Show under violations that may result in
immediate termination without prior warning. This template should default to **Termination
Review**, not a warning, and should never be issued without owner approval.

**Also relevant (§2U):**
> Employees may never leave a client without ensuring appropriate coverage or receiving
> authorization from the office.

---

## 3. Failure to communicate

**Policy (handbook §2N, Communication):**
> Employees should: Return calls and messages promptly. Notify the office of concerns
> immediately. Report changes in client condition. Ask questions when unsure. Maintain a
> respectful tone. Communicate honestly.

> Never assume someone else has already reported a concern.

**On going around the office (§2N):**
> Refer questions regarding scheduling, billing, care plans, or medical concerns to the office

**Phone requirement (Section 2):**
> Employees must maintain a working telephone capable of receiving calls and text messages.

**Checkboxes:** ☐ Did not return office calls ☐ Did not return office texts ☐ Did not report
a scheduling problem ☐ Did not report a change in client condition ☐ Did not report an
incident ☐ Arranged schedule changes directly with the client or family ☐ Other

**Why it matters:** the office cannot coordinate care, document concerns or respond to
emergencies it does not know about. Arranging things directly with a family also leaves the
client with no record and no backup if the arrangement fails.

## 4. Unprofessional conduct

**Policy (handbook §2M, Professionalism):**
> Employees are expected to conduct themselves with honesty, integrity, compassion, and
> respect at all times.

> Professional behavior includes: Being courteous. Speaking respectfully. Maintaining
> appropriate boundaries. Being dependable. Following company policies. Demonstrating
> patience. Respecting client choices. Maintaining confidentiality.

**Also (§2M), which covers a lot of real incidents:**
> Employees should avoid: Becoming personally involved in family conflicts. Sharing excessive
> personal information. Accepting inappropriate gifts. Making promises they cannot keep.
> Discussing company matters with clients. Speaking negatively about coworkers or previous
> caregivers.

**Checkboxes:** ☐ Disrespectful to office staff ☐ Disrespectful to a coworker ☐ Disrespectful
to a client or family ☐ Spoke negatively about coworkers or previous caregivers ☐ Discussed
company matters with a client ☐ Inappropriate language ☐ Other

## 5. Insubordination

**Policy (handbook §2V)** lists *Insubordination* among behaviors that may result in
corrective action.

**Plain wording for the notice**, since "insubordination" is not a word every caregiver will
read the same way:

> Failure or refusal to follow reasonable directions, instructions, or policies from
> supervisors or agency leadership.

**Checkboxes:** ☐ Refused to perform assigned duties ☐ Refused to follow a care plan after
being directed ☐ Refused to complete required documentation ☐ Argued with or hung up on
office staff ☐ Ignored scheduling instructions ☐ Other

---

## 6. Client boundaries and gifts  ⚠ corrected

**Policy (handbook §4Q, Gifts & Gratuities). Quote it accurately:**
> Many clients express appreciation through gifts or tips. While small tokens of appreciation
> may occasionally be appropriate, employees should never request or expect gifts.

> Employees may NOT: Ask clients for money. Request gifts. Solicit donations. Pressure clients
> to purchase items. Accept expensive gifts or valuable property.

> If a client offers: Large amounts of cash, Jewelry, Vehicles, Real estate, Valuable
> collectibles, Other significant gifts — politely decline the gift and notify the office.

**Boundaries (handbook §2M):** the "Employees should avoid" list quoted in template 4 above.

**Checkboxes:** ☐ Asked a client for money ☐ Requested or solicited a gift ☐ Accepted an
expensive gift or valuable property ☐ Did not decline and report a significant gift
☐ Became personally involved in family conflicts ☐ Shared excessive personal information
☐ Made promises that could not be kept ☐ Other

⚠ **Do not use this template for a small token of appreciation.** Under §4Q that is not a
violation. If the concern is a pattern rather than a single gift, say so explicitly in the
incident description.

## 7. Improper phone use

**Policy (handbook §2P, Personal Cell Phone Use):**
> Employees are expected to limit personal phone use while providing services.

> Employees should not: Spend excessive time texting. Browse social media during work hours.
> Watch videos or stream media. Play games. Make lengthy personal phone calls.

Worth pairing with the permitted-use list from §2P, so the notice is fair: AxisCare, EVV,
care plans, care notes, work communication and emergencies are all fine.
